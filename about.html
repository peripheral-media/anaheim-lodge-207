About Our Lodge

Chartered October 14, 1870 — Orange County's oldest Masonic lodge, and still a home for good men and their families.

Since 1870

More Than 150 Years in the Heart of Anaheim

Anaheim Lodge No. 207 received its charter on October 14, 1870, making it the oldest continuously operating Masonic lodge in Orange County. Our lodge was meeting before Orange County itself existed, and through every era since, its members have been builders of this city — business owners, teachers, first responders, veterans, tradesmen and civic leaders.

Today we are a family lodge in the truest sense. Our members come from many cultures, faiths and professions, and our wives, children and friends join us for dinners, celebrations and service projects throughout the year. What unites us is a shared belief that character matters, and that a man is measured by how he treats his family, his neighbors and his community.

That belief shows up in the work we do, from our long-standing partnership with Benjamin Franklin Elementary School to the quiet ways brothers support one another in hard times. We invite you to become part of the story.

What is Freemasonry?

Freemasonry is the world's oldest and largest fraternity — a brotherhood that helps good men become better through lessons in morality, self-improvement and service. It is neither a religion nor a secret society. Men of every background and faith meet as equals, leave politics and sectarian debate at the door, and focus on what they share.

Our lessons are taught through symbols and stories drawn from the ancient stonemasons — the square reminds us to act honestly, the compasses to keep our passions within bounds. They invite reflection rather than dictate belief, and they are meant to be carried home and lived every day.

The Four Cardinal Virtues

Every Mason is taught four ancient virtues. They shape what happens inside the lodge room — and, more importantly, how we live outside it.

Temperance

Self-control in word and deed. The discipline to keep our habits, tempers and appetites in balance, so we can be present for the people who count on us.

Prudence

Wisdom in action. Thinking before we speak, choosing carefully, and steering our lives by reason and good judgment rather than impulse.

Fortitude

Courage to do what is right when it is hard. Standing firm through trials, keeping our word, and lifting up a brother when he needs it most.

Justice

Fairness to all. Treating every person with respect and honesty, at home, at work and in the community we serve.
